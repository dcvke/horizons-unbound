# Convergence

> I had made four columns in the notebook. It seemed a sensible number until two storms met.

## When storms meet

Storms can converge and merge, combining the effects of two kinds. Recognising a single colour is no longer enough preparation for a mixed storm. A plan that works against one effect may place you in the path of the other.

By default, there is a **30% chance** for a second storm of another kind to rise near an existing one a few minutes later. The pair move towards each other and can merge. `secondStormChance` controls this additional natural event; setting it to zero disables that chance.

This is separate from the ordinary interval between storms. It does not mean there is always another storm waiting whenever you see one. A server or pack may also change which kinds can appear naturally.

The operator command `/storm twin` asks for a second storm beside an existing one, but needs an eligible storm and a suitable location. It can fail when no suitable setup is available. `/storm info` helps you inspect the current dimension before trying again.

## The Great Storm

Encountering all four kinds leads towards the Great Storm and its finale. The mod tracks related advancements, including `all_kinds` and `grand`. Cutscenes are presentation; advancement progression is still supported when they are disabled.

The finale is deliberately left out of the main survival entries. If you want to stage it rather than discover it, an operator can use `/storm grand` in a test world. That command is an administrative demonstration, not an extra survival crafting recipe or a hidden rite.

## The traveller's explanation

The journal proposes that the four storms are places touching the same weak point in the world. The Great Storm would be that point giving way. It is a story the traveller uses to make sense of what they see, not a second progression system that the player has to solve.

There is a page after the account of the convergence. It has no sketch, no measurements and no date. Just a pencilled line, pressed hard enough to mark the sheet beneath it:

*If the horizon opens, do not assume it is opening away from you.*
