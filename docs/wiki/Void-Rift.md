# Void Rift

> There was someone on the ridge wearing my coat. I raised my hand. They had already lowered theirs.

The purple storm is usually the first one people try to photograph. Distance makes it look clean and orderly: rings of light, a tall column, a sky pulled into long seams. Close to it, a familiar route becomes difficult to trust.

## What it does

The End effect set includes player echoes, spatial seams, distance jumps, stalled projectiles and gravity waves. Low-gravity behaviour changes how movement feels inside the storm. A seam can carry you across space rather than simply marking a line in the air.

An echo is part of the storm's effects. Treat it as a sign that the area is distorted, rather than as another player you can safely follow. Arrows suspended around the column also make poor evidence that a fight has ended. Position and movement deserve more attention here than they would in ordinary rain.

End storms can reshape eligible natural terrain and bring fragments from the End when those systems are enabled. The default creature list includes endermen and endermites. Creature spawning has its own settings and does not operate in Peaceful.

## Finding a steady place

A placed [Reality Anchor](Anchors-and-Hot-Crust.md) creates an eight-block spherical area in which the relevant End distortions are suppressed. Its boundary is indicated by particles. The radius is measured in three dimensions: a block eight levels above your head is not equivalent to one beside your feet.

An anchor is a local refuge. It does not dismiss the storm or clear the whole landscape. Keep its position in mind before moving beyond the boundary, particularly when gravity or distance has become unreliable.
